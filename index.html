There is a symbol you have almost certainly seen, though perhaps you have never truly looked at it. It appears on the back of the American one-dollar bill, pressed into the base of an unfinished pyramid. Most people call it a conspiracy, a relic, or they ignore it altogether.

I could not move on because I came to understand that this symbol is not merely a piece of national iconography or a relic of eighteenth-century political theater. It is an instruction manual. A map. A call to a kind of work that my ancestors understood, and that my generation had almost completely forgotten.

For as long as I can remember, I have been drawn to the places where official history grows thin. Where the story told in textbooks runs out of explanations, where the gaps open wider the more you look. The pyramids were not built by slaves. They were engineered by masters, by architects, by people who understood mathematics and physics and sacred geometry at a level that should have been impossible for their time.

What I found disturbed me and exhilarated me in equal measure. The builders of the great pyramids were not aliens. They were not slaves driven by whips. They were us. They were Black people. They were African. They were brilliant, organized, and absolutely sovereign. And we have been hiding that truth for centuries because someone decided we should forget who we were.
